## Part 1

### Friends

Questions:

- Do you have a friend you have known for a long time?

  > Yes. My closest friend is someone I met on my first day of primary school.
  >
  > We've maintained our friendship for over two decades, navigating different schools, universities, and cities. That long shared history has created an unbreakable bond and a deep intuitive understanding of each other.

- Do you often go out with your friends?

  > I would say we have a good balance. We make a point to schedule proper outings like trying a new restaurant or going to an exhibition a couple of times a month.
  >
  > But we also value simpler, more frequent interactions, like a quick coffee or a phone call, which are just as important for maintaining our connection.

- Do you prefer to spend time with one friend or with a group of friends?

  > I appreciate both dynamics for different reasons.
  >
  > One on one time allows for deeper, more intimate and vulnerable conversations.
  >
  > Group settings, however, are fantastic for energy, laughter and shared experiences. I don't have a strong preference.
  >
  > It depends entirely on my mood and what I'm seeking from the social interaction.

- How important are friends to you?

  > They are utterly vital. My friends are my chosen family.
  >
  > They provide a crucial support system, emotional sustenance, and shared joy. They offer different perspectives, honest advice, and unconditional support through life's challenges. Making the good times better and the hard times easier to bear.

- Is there a difference between where you meet friends now and where you used to meet them in the past?

  > There's a significant difference in our teens and early 20s. We almost exclusively met in public places like shopping malls, cinemas, or fast food restaurants.
  >
  > Now, as adults, we gravitate much more towards each other's homes for dinner, or quieter cafes and pubs where we can actually hear each other talk.

- What do you usually do with your friends?

  > Our activities are quite varied. We often go out for meals or coffee to catch up, go for walks in the park or watch films together. Sometimes our meet UPS are even more low key. Just spending an evening, cooking dinner together and talking at home is just as enjoyable.

- Where do you often meet each other?

  > We tend to favor casual, comfortable environments where we can talk without shouting.
  >
  > Our most common meeting spots are cozy cafes, each other's homes or parks when the weather is nice.
  >
  > The location is always chosen for its ability to facilitate good conversation.

- Why are some places suitable for meeting while others are not?

  > Suitability hinges on the desired interaction. A noisy sports bar is perfect for watching a match with a group, but terrible for a heart to heart conversation. A library is suitable for studying together, but not for a celebratory party.
  >
  > The ideal venue matches the group size and the primary goal of the meeting be it lively socializing or intimate talking.

- Would you invite friends to your home?

  > Absolutely. I consider it the highest form of hospitality and intimacy.
  >
  > Inviting someone into your personal space is a sign of trust and comfort.
  >
  > Some of my best memories with friends are of informal gatherings at home, cooking together, playing games, and talking for hours without the time constraints of a public venue.
