Part 1

Friends

Questions:

  • Do you have a friend you have known for a long time?

    Yes. My closest friend is someone I met on my first day of primary school.

    We’ve maintained our friendship for over two decades, navigating different schools, universities, and cities. That long shared history has created an unbreakable bond and a deep intuitive understanding of each other.

  • Do you often go out with your friends?

    I would say we have a good balance. We make a point to schedule proper outings like trying a new restaurant or going to an exhibition a couple of times a month.

    But we also value simpler, more frequent interactions, like a quick coffee or a phone call, which are just as important for maintaining our connection.

  • Do you prefer to spend time with one friend or with a group of friends?

    I appreciate both dynamics for different reasons.

    One on one time allows for deeper, more intimate and vulnerable conversations.

    Group settings, however, are fantastic for energy, laughter and shared experiences. I don’t have a strong preference.

    It depends entirely on my mood and what I’m seeking from the social interaction.

  • How important are friends to you?

    They are utterly vital. My friends are my chosen family.

    They provide a crucial support system, emotional sustenance, and shared joy. They offer different perspectives, honest advice, and unconditional support through life’s challenges. Making the good times better and the hard times easier to bear.

  • Is there a difference between where you meet friends now and where you used to meet them in the past?

    There’s a significant difference in our teens and early 20s. We almost exclusively met in public places like shopping malls, cinemas, or fast food restaurants.

    Now, as adults, we gravitate much more towards each other’s homes for dinner, or quieter cafes and pubs where we can actually hear each other talk.

  • What do you usually do with your friends?

    Our activities are quite varied. We often go out for meals or coffee to catch up, go for walks in the park or watch films together. Sometimes our meet UPS are even more low key. Just spending an evening, cooking dinner together and talking at home is just as enjoyable.

  • Where do you often meet each other?

    We tend to favor casual, comfortable environments where we can talk without shouting.

    Our most common meeting spots are cozy cafes, each other’s homes or parks when the weather is nice.

    The location is always chosen for its ability to facilitate good conversation.

  • Why are some places suitable for meeting while others are not?

    Suitability hinges on the desired interaction. A noisy sports bar is perfect for watching a match with a group, but terrible for a heart to heart conversation. A library is suitable for studying together, but not for a celebratory party.

    The ideal venue matches the group size and the primary goal of the meeting be it lively socializing or intimate talking.

  • Would you invite friends to your home?

    Absolutely. I consider it the highest form of hospitality and intimacy.

    Inviting someone into your personal space is a sign of trust and comfort.

    Some of my best memories with friends are of informal gatherings at home, cooking together, playing games, and talking for hours without the time constraints of a public venue.

Advertisement

Questions:

  • Do you like advertisements?

    I appreciate well crafted creative advertisements that tell a story or artistically executed. However, I have a strong aversion to intrusive, repetitive, or blatantly manipulative ads. The sheer volume of advertising we are exposed to daily is overwhelming.

    So the ones that earn my attention are those that offer something of value, be it, humour, beauty, or useful Information.

  • Do you often see advertisements when you are on your phone or computer?

    Yes. I frequently encounter advertisements while using my phone or computer. In fact, digital ads are almost unavoidable in China, appearing on social media platforms within apps and as pre roll videos.

    While some can be intrusive, I find many useful, especially those tailored to my interests.

    As they often introduce me to new products or services, I might genuinely like.

  • Do you see a lot of advertising on trains or other transport？

    An enormous amount. Public transport is a prime advertising, real estate, because it captures a captive audience with little else to do during their commute, the interiors and exteriors of buses and trains, as well as stations themselves, are plastered with posters and digital screens promoting everything from new movies to university°.

  • Is there an advertisement that made an impression on you when you were a child?

    Yes. I vividly remember a series of animated commercials for breakfast cereal.

    They featured a catchy Jingle and a colorful cartoon mascot going on adventures, the combination of bright animation music, and the promise of a Free toy inside made it incredibly memorable and effective on my childhood self.

  • What kind of advertising do you like?

    I’m most drawn to minimalist, witty or emotionally intelligent advertising, a clever print AD that uses a play on words, or a short film style AD that tells a genuine story without hard selling a product stands out. I dislike loud, aggressive sales pitches. I prefer ads that respect the audiences, Intelligence and seek to engage rather than just shout at them.

  • Have you ever bought something because of an advertisement?

    Yes, I have last year I bought a facial cleanser because I saw an influencer recommending it on du yan. The idea made it look amazing, but after using it, I found it was just average. Now I’m more careful and read reviews before buying.

Shoes

Questions:

  • Do you like buying shoes? How often?

    I do, but I am a considered shopper rather than an impulsive one.

    I probably add a new pair to my wardrobe, every 8 to 12 months, usually to replace a worn out staple or for a specific purpose, like a new pair of running trainers. I see them as a functional investment rather than a frequent fashion purchase.

  • Have you ever bought shoes online?

    I have. But I approach it with caution. I tend to only buy from brands whose sizing I’m already familiar with, or from retailers with a very clear and Free returns policy.

    The inability to try them on for comfort and fit is a significant drawback.

    So it requires a bit of research and a leap of faith.

  • How much money do you usually spend on shoes?

    It varies dramatically depending on the purpose. I’m happy to spend a significant amount on a high quality, versatile pair of leather boots or professional shoes that will last for years, for casual summer footwear, like sandals.

    I’m much more budget conscious. On average. I’d say I spend a moderate amount prioritizing value over price.

  • Which do you prefer, fashionable shoes or comfortable shoes?

    Comfort wins without question. I firmly believe there’s no need for these to be mutually exclusive.

    As many brands now successfully merge style with ergonomics. However, if forced to choose, I’d always opt for comfort.

    There’s little point in wearing stylish shoes if you’re in too much pain to enjoy yourself or walk confidently.

Names

  • How do you remember people’s names?

    I usually repeat the person’s name immediately after hearing it and try to use it in conversation. I also associate the name with a facial feature or a scene.

    Sometimes I save it in my phone with a small note.

  • How do you feel when people can’t remember your name?

    I would feel a little awkward or disappointed, especially if we’ve met more than once, it might seem like I didn’t leave an impression, but I also understand, because I forget names, too. So I usually just smile and reintroduce myself.

  • Do you often forget people’s names?

    Yes, I often forget people’s names, especially when I meet them for the first time I might remember the face clearly, but the name just disappears. I think it’s because I’m not paying full attention during introductions.

    There’s often a sense of innocence and wonder with plenty of time for play and exploration. However, as an adult, there’s a different kind of excitement in taking on responsibilities, pursuing personal goals and experiencing a greater sense of independence.

    Additionally, I enjoy taking up creative projects, such as writing or experimenting with photography.

    These activities not only keep me occupied, but also provide a sense of accomplishment.

  • When would you feel bored?

    I usually find myself feeling bored when I’m not mentally stimulated, or when I have a lot of Free time with nothing specific to do. For example, during long waits or when there’s a lack of interesting activities, I might start to feel a bit bored.

    However, i’ve learned to combat this by planning my days effectively and filling my Free time with activities that I enjoy, whether it’s reading a book, working on a personal project or engaging in a hobby.

    Staying proactive helps me ward off boredom and make the most of my time.

    Physical newspapers are rarely read now, especially among young people most get news from apps like Taobao or work at.

    Feeds. However, some older people still keep the habit of reading a paper copy in the morning, but overall digital news has almost completely replaced traditional newspapers where I live.

  • Do people prefer local or international news?

    It depends on the person. Most ordinary people care more about local news because it directly affects their daily life.

    Like transport and policies. However, younger and more educated people follow international news closely, too, especially about technology and global events, local news feels practical.

    While international news provides a broader world view.

  • Do you think it’s important to have a national identity?

    Yes, I think having a national identity is important, it gives people a sense of belonging and shared history in a globalized world.

    It helps preserve unique cultures and traditions. It also unites citizens during crises like natural disasters. However, it should not turn into extreme nationalism that rejects everything foreign.

  • How can people develop their national identity?

    People can develop national identity by learning history and understanding the sacrifices made by past generations.

    Participating in traditional festivals and cultural activities also deepens the connection traveling within the country and appreciating its diverse landscape helps too.

    Most importantly, contributing to the community through small daily acts makes you feel truly part of the nation.

遇到困难终成功的人

Questions:

  • In your country, what industry is it easier to be successful in?

    In China, I think the technology and internet industry offers the most opportunities for success. Right now, the market is huge.

    And there is strong government support, e commerce, short videos, and AI are booming. However, competition is extremely fierce. So you still need innovation and hard work.

  • What’s the difference between ordinary people and successful people?

    Ordinary people often do what is required.

    While successful people go the extra mile, successful people are also more willing to take risks and learn from failure. They tend to have a clearer vision and stronger self discipline. Most importantly, they don’t wait for opportunities, they create them.

  • What are the factors leading to people’s success?

    Many factors lead to success, hard work and persistence are the foundation, because without them, talent is wasted, good timing and luck.

    Also play a part like being in the right industry at the right time, strong social connections and family support often open doors. However, the most crucial factor is the ability to learn and adapt quickly.
